# One-Line Roast candidates — the gadget I use every day

Judge criteria, in order: (1) clearly roasts a daily gadget/app, (2) actually funny on first read, (3) fresh angle, (4) neatly worded, tight, (5) one person's specific gripe with a concrete detail. Crudeness doesn't count.

## Candidates

1. "My smartwatch congratulates me for 10,000 steps a day, nine thousand of which are just the walk from the couch to the fridge."
   — Works: names a daily gadget, concrete behaviour (step counting), sting of recognition. Fails a little: the "steps to the fridge" gag is a known meme shape, so freshness is only moderate.

2. "My phone goes from 40% to dead the moment I actually need directions, like it panics at responsibility."
   — Works: specific, relatable battery behaviour. Fails: "phone dies when you need it" is the single most obvious phone complaint; freshness is weak.

3. "My earbuds cancel noise so well I can hear my own thoughts, and honestly, the traffic was better company."
   — Works: fresh twist on a praised feature, neat turn at the end. Fails: the target behaviour (noise cancelling working well) is barely a gripe; the roast is aimed at my own thoughts, not the gadget.

4. "My smart TV needs a software update before it will play a show about people living without electricity."
   — Works: concrete detail, neat irony. Fails: "smart TV wants an update" is one of the three examples in the bounty description itself, so freshness is poor.

5. "My alarm's snooze button is so generous it should be paying rent and doing the dishes."
   — Works: short, punchy, personifies the button. Fails: the gripe is really about my own snoozing, not a behaviour of the gadget; the gadget is doing exactly what it was asked.

6. "My phone's autocorrect has started three arguments I never meant to have."
   — Works: specific, personal, funny premise. Fails: autocorrect roasts are well-worn territory; the line is a bit bare — no concrete detail of how it actually behaves.

7. "My router reboots itself at 3 a.m. every night, like the internet needs its beauty sleep more than I do."
   — Works: concrete behaviour, fresh personification, neat. Fails: a router is borderline "gadget you use every day" — most people never think about it; the sting is mild.

8. "My fitness app celebrates a rest day like I climbed a mountain, then silently judges the sandwich."
   — Works: two-beat structure, specific app behaviour. Fails: slightly long and the second beat is doing most of the work; "judges my food" is a familiar fitness-app joke.

9. "My voice assistant understands 'play jazz' perfectly and hears 'turn off the lights' as a mere suggestion."
   — Works: very specific, true-to-life behaviour, neat contrast. Fails: the payoff ("a mere suggestion") is a little dry — it reads clever rather than funny.

10. "My phone says storage is full, then offers to delete the photos I took five minutes ago to make room for its own update."
    — Works: concrete, infuriating, specific sequence of behaviours. Fails: "storage full" is a common complaint; the line is long and the joke lands late.

## Pick

**Winner: candidate 1, the smartwatch.**

It wins on the judge's order: it names a daily gadget outright, the joke lands on first read (the watch praising you for doing nothing), it has a concrete, specific detail (step counting, couch-to-fridge trips), and it is tightly worded with a clean setup–punchline shape. Its one weakness is freshness — the fridge-steps gag is familiar — so the final line should sharpen the detail rather than repeat the meme plainly: make the number exact and the reward absurd, so the watch sounds like an overenthusiastic coach praising a commute to the snacks.

**Picked line (draft):** "My smartwatch congratulates me for 10,000 steps a day, nine thousand of which are just the walk from the couch to the fridge."
